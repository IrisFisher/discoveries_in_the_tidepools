What Could You See at the Tidepools?

Different creatures live at different distances from the shore and can be seen at different times based on how low the tide is. We call the different levels of ocean the tidal zones. It's best to go tidepooling when the tide is very low, so that you have access to all of the tidal zones.

Tidal Zone Description Animals
High intertidal zone The zone which floods daily during high tides but is exposed most of the time. Animals that tolerate both crashing waves and being dry for long stretches of time, including barnacles, marine snails, mussels, limpets, shore crabs and hermit crabs.
Middle intertidal zones The zone which the tides cover and uncover twice per day. (There's a lot of great tidepooling here!) Animals which can tolerate being regularly exposed and submerged including sea stars and sea anenomes.
Low intertidal zone The zone which is submerged most of the year and only exposed during the very lowest tides. Animals which spend most/all of their lives underwater including nudibranchs and brittle stars.

Source: “What Is the Intertidal Zone?” National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration, oceanservice.noaa.gov/facts/intertidal-zone.html.
