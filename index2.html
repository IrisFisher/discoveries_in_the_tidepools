Planning Your Visit to the Tidepools

Tidepools are formed when the tide recedes and water is trapped in the dips and crevices of rocks, forming pools. So, choose a rocky beach for the best experience. I've had great tidepooling experiences in Northern California at Salt Point State Park in Sonoma County, JV Fitzgerald Marine Reserve in San Mateo Country, and MacKerricher State Park in Mendocino. In Southern California, I've read that some good options are Point Loma in San Diego, Laguna and Newport beaches in Orange County, and Abalone Cove Shoreline Park in Los Angeles County. But if you're near the ocean, just look up a good spot nearby.

The lower the tide the more you see so use a tide chart to figure our when you're going to go. (It's recommeneded to go tidepooling at tides of 1.5 feet or lower).

Be prepared! This is my go-to tidepooling gear checklist:

  • Warm wool or sythetic shirt and pants. Not cotton if you can avoid it (cotton is cold when wet)!
  • Warm wool or sythetic coat.
  • Closed-toed shoes with traction that you're okay with getting wet (water shoes, old sneakers, etc.).
  • Wool socks.
  • Phone or water resistant camera.
  • Change of clothes/socks/shoes.

Know tidepooling etiquette! Being aware and respectful of your surroundings is crucial for keeping you and the creatures you're appreciating safe.
